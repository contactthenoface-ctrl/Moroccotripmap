Welcome to Dakhla — lagoon, wind and the edge of the desert

Dakhla sits on a long peninsula in southern Morocco, between a vast turquoise lagoon and the open Atlantic. Trade winds, flat water and empty shores have made it one of Africa’s leading spots for kitesurf and wingfoil — but the place is more than a sports camp.

Beyond the water: the White Dune rising from the lagoon, Dragon Island’s rocky silhouette, oyster farms on the water, Foum El Bouir on the ocean side, and desert tracks toward Sebkha Imlili. Town life is calm; most days revolve around wind, light and the horizon.

Three to five days work well if you mix water time with one dune outing and a slower lagoon evening. Summer is windy and warm; winter is milder and still excellent for sports.

Practical tips for Dakhla

Getting around

Airport transfers and camp shuttles cover most needs. Agree taxi prices before longer lagoon or desert tracks.

Wind & sun

The trade winds are strong. Use high SPF, a windbreaker and plenty of water even when the air feels cool.

Cash & bookings

Carry dirhams for taxis and small stops. Book kite camps and lessons ahead in peak season.

Good to know

Dakhla travel FAQ

How many days in Dakhla?

Three days cover lagoon orientation, one sport session, White Dune and Foum El Bouir. Five days suit serious kitesurfers.

Is Dakhla only for kitesurf?

Wind sports are the main draw, but the lagoon, White Dune, Dragon Island, oysters and desert edge work for non-riders too.

How do I get to Dakhla?

Fly into Dakhla Airport (VIL). Domestic flights link from Casablanca and other cities; overland is a long southern road journey.

Best time to visit?

Wind is present much of the year. October to June is popular for comfortable temperatures and reliable sessions.
