Nejjarine Museum

A restored funduq that celebrates Morocco's woodworking, with a rooftop view over the medina.

Best Time to GoMorning or Afternoon
Time NeededAbout 1 Hour
EntryMuseum Admission Charged
WherePlace Nejjarine, Fes el-Bali

A Caravanserai Turned Woodworking Museum

The Nejjarine Museum of Wooden Arts and Crafts occupies a beautifully restored funduq, an 18th-century inn where merchants once lodged with their animals and goods. The building itself, with its galleries, carved cedar and tiled courtyard, is as much an exhibit as the collection inside.

Inside, tools, furniture, doors and everyday objects explain how Moroccan woodworkers shaped cedar and other woods, which is useful context for everything you see in the medina. Finish on the rooftop café for a view across the rooftops. The carved fountain on Place Nejjarine just outside is also worth a moment.

Practical Tips for Visiting Nejjarine Museum

Getting There

It stands on Place Nejjarine in Fes el-Bali, a short walk from Bou Inania and Talaa Kebira. Follow the signs to the fountain.

Timing

Usually open in the daytime, with closed hours that vary. Allow about one hour, including the rooftop.

Good to Know

Admission is charged, so carry some cash. Combine it with Bou Inania and Al Quaraouiyine for a compact heritage morning.

Nejjarine Museum FAQ

What is the Nejjarine Museum?

A museum of Moroccan woodworking in a restored 18th-century funduq in Fes el-Bali.

How long do you need at the Nejjarine Museum?

About an hour, including the rooftop café.

Is there an entrance fee?

Yes, admission is charged. Check the current rate locally.

Is the rooftop worth it?

Yes, for a calm break and a view across the medina rooftops.
