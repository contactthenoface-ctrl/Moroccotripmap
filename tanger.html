Welcome to Tangier — gateway between Europe and Africa

Tangier sits where the Atlantic meets the Mediterranean, a short hop from Spain across the Strait of Gibraltar. The medina climbs toward the kasbah; below, the port and new town open onto corniche and beach. Ferries, the high-speed Al Boraq train and Ibn Battuta Airport (TNG) make it one of Morocco’s easiest northern arrivals.

Walk the medina and Petit Socco, pause at Café Hafa for strait views, then head west to Cap Spartel and the Hercules Caves, or east to Cap Malabata. The American Legation museum anchors a quieter chapter of the old town. Day trips reach Asilah’s walls, Tetouan’s medina or the blue lanes of Chefchaouen.

Planning

Suggested 3-day plan for Tangier

Medina and kasbah first, then western capes, then beach or a day trip.

Morning — Grand Socco & medina

Enter through the Grand Socco, wander toward Petit Socco and the lower medina lanes.

Afternoon — Kasbah Museum

Climb to the kasbah for the museum, gardens and strait views over the roofs.

Evening — Café Hafa or port

Mint tea on Hafa’s terraces, or seafood near the port before returning to your stay.

Morning — Cap Spartel

Lighthouse and cliffs where Atlantic and Mediterranean currents meet.

Afternoon — Hercules Caves

Short stop at the sea caves and the famous opening toward the ocean.

Evening — Medina dinner

Back in town for a medina restaurant or a quieter new-town option.

Morning — Tangier Beach or Cap Malabata

Easy morning on the city beach / corniche, or east to Cap Malabata for open views.

Afternoon — American Legation or free time

Visit the American Legation museum in the medina, or keep time for shopping and cafés.

Evening — Departure options

Train or road links toward Rabat, Chefchaouen or the airport depending on your route.

Practical tips

Practical tips for Tangier

Getting around

Petit taxis in town; agree fares to Cap Spartel, the caves or Malabata. Walking covers medina and kasbah.

Strait weather

Winds can be strong on the capes — a layer helps even in mild seasons.

Cash and cards

Cards in larger hotels and restaurants; dirhams for taxis, small cafés and medina shops.

Good to know

FAQ about Tangier

How many days in Tangier?

Two full days cover medina, kasbah, Café Hafa and Cap Spartel with the caves. A third day fits Malabata, the Legation or a day trip.

Is Tangier only a transit city?

It is a gateway, but the medina, strait viewpoints and nearby capes reward a proper stay — not only a ferry connection.

How do I get to Tangier?

Ibn Battuta Airport (TNG), Al Boraq high-speed train from Casablanca/Rabat/Kenitra, long-distance trains and buses, plus ferries from Spain.

Best time to visit Tangier?

Spring and autumn for mild walking weather. Summer is busy; winter is cooler and windier on the exposed capes.
