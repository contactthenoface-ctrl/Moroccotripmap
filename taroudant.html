Taroudant

Intact ochre ramparts, unhurried souks, and the Souss Valley between the High Atlas and the Anti-Atlas.

Best time to visit

October – May

Ideal stay

2 – 3 days

Vibe

Authentic & relaxed

Nearest airport

Agadir Al Massira (AGA)

Welcome to the walled city of the Souss

Taroudant sits in the fertile Souss Valley, framed by the High Atlas to the north and the Anti-Atlas to the south. Its 7.5 km of continuous 16th-century adobe ramparts remain among the most complete in Morocco, and the medina inside them is still a working market town rather than a museum piece.

Whether you circle the walls by calèche at sunset, bargain for Berber silver in the souks, or day-trip to the palm oasis of Tiout, Taroudant offers a slower, more authentic southern Morocco experience.

Planning

Suggested 3-day itinerary

A balanced plan to see the highlights without rushing.

Morning — Place Assarag & ramparts

Start at Place Assarag for coffee and orientation, then walk or take a calèche along a stretch of the ramparts while the light is soft.

Afternoon — Arab & Berber souks

Wander both markets without a rigid plan. Look for silver, leather and spices; the pace is unhurried.

Evening — Sunset on the walls

Return to the ramparts for the golden hour, then dinner in a riad or a simple restaurant near the square.

Morning — Kasbah & Bab el-Kasbah

Explore the quieter kasbah quarter and the monumental Bab el-Kasbah gate; climb where allowed for views.

Afternoon — Palais Claudio Bravo or tanneries

Reserve a visit to the Claudio Bravo palace-museum, or watch traditional tanning near Bab Targhount.

Evening — Quiet riad dinner

Book a table in a medina riad for home-style Moroccan cooking away from the busiest lanes.

Morning — Tiout Oasis or free time

Drive or join a tour to Tiout for palm groves and a hilltop kasbah, or stay in town for hammam and argan cooperatives.

Afternoon — Return or Anti-Atlas foothills

On the way back, stop at a women’s argan cooperative if you have not already; otherwise revisit favourite souk alleys.

Evening — Last night in Taroudant

Mint tea on a rooftop and an early night if you leave for Agadir or the Atlas the next morning.

Practical tips for visiting Taroudant

Getting around

The medina is compact and best explored on foot. For the full ramparts circuit, hire a calèche (horse-drawn carriage) and agree the price before you start. Petit taxis serve the town; always ask for the meter.

Souk bargaining

Shopping is calmer than in larger tourist cities. Start around half the asking price, stay friendly, and walk away if needed — vendors here are used to local trade as much as visitors.

Stay hydrated

Summers in the Souss Valley are hot. Drink bottled water, seek shade in the souks at midday, and save longer walks on the ramparts for morning or late afternoon.

Beyond the city

Best day trips from Taroudant

Rent a car, book a driver, or join a small group to reach these landscapes in a single day.

Tiout Oasis

Palm groves, a hilltop kasbah and quiet village life about 30 minutes south-east.

Anti-Atlas foothills

Berber villages, argan forests and rugged red-earth landscapes within easy reach.

Taliouine saffron

Morocco’s saffron heartland east of Taroudant, with cooperatives and mountain views.

Agadir coast

Atlantic beaches and the modern city about 1–1.5 hours west on the N10.

Good to know

Taroudant travel FAQ

How many days should I spend in Taroudant?

Two to three days is ideal: one for the medina, walls and souks, one for the kasbah and a museum or cooperative, and optionally a day for Tiout oasis or the Anti-Atlas.

Is Taroudant safe for tourists?

Yes. Taroudant is a quieter market town with a strong local character. Use the same common-sense precautions as in any medina: keep valuables secure and agree taxi fares in advance if the meter is not used.

What currency is used and can I pay by card?

Morocco uses the dirham (MAD). Cards are accepted in many hotels and some restaurants; keep cash for the souks, calèches, petit taxis and small vendors.

How do I get from Agadir airport to Taroudant?

Agadir Al Massira (AGA) is about 60–80 km west (roughly 1–1.5 hours by road). Private transfers, shared grands taxis and buses serve the route; book a transfer in advance if you arrive late.
