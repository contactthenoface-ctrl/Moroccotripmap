Fez

Morocco's Spiritual Capital, a Medieval Medina Still Very Much Alive.

Best Time to Visit Mar – May, Sep – Nov
Ideal Stay 2 – 3 Days
Top Vibe Medieval & Artisanal
Nearest Airport FEZ (Saïss)

A City That Never Really Modernized Its Old Town, and Is Better For It

Fes el Bali, the walled old city, is often described as the largest car-free urban zone on the planet. That single fact shapes everything about a visit here: goods still move by mule and handcart through more than 9,000 alleys, and entire trades - dyers, tanners, coppersmiths, weavers - operate more or less as they have for centuries, in the same fondouks their guilds have used since the Marinid era.

Founded in the 9th century and home to what UNESCO and Guinness World Records both recognize as the oldest continuously operating university on Earth, Fez carries a different weight than Marrakech or Casablanca. It rewards travelers willing to get a little lost, and it is, without much competition, Morocco's best city for understanding how the country's craft traditions actually survived.

Suggested Route

A 3-Day Route Through Fes el Bali

Bab Boujloud to the Tanneries

Start at Bab Boujloud early, before the alleys fill up. Walk down Talaa Kebira, the medina's main artery, stopping at Bou Inania Madrasa on the way.

Continue toward the Chouara Tannery in the late morning, when the dye pits are being actively worked. Most leather shops around it will hand you a sprig of mint to counter the smell - accept it, you will need it.

End the afternoon at Al Quaraouiyine and the Nejjarine fountain and museum, both a short walk from the tanneries.

The Mellah, Royal Palace & Sunset at the Tombs

Head to the Mellah in the morning to see the Ibn Danan Synagogue and the distinctive wooden-balcony houses, then walk past the closed but photogenic gates of the Royal Palace (Dar el-Makhzen).

Spend the middle of the day at Dar Batha Museum, a quieter stop that most visitors skip.

Take a petit taxi up to the Merenid Tombs an hour before sunset. It is the single best view of the medina, and largely free of the crowding you get at rooftop cafés.

Slow Morning, Then a Half-Day Trip

Use the morning to revisit anything you rushed, or to browse the souks properly - ceramics near Ain Nokbi, textiles and babouches around the kissarias.

In the afternoon, take one of the day trips below rather than trying to cram in more of the medina; by day three, most travelers are ready for open air and a change of pace.

Fassi Cuisine

Where to Eat in Fez

Fassi cooking is considered by many Moroccans to be the country's most refined - pastilla and rfissa are said to have originated here.

Practical Tips for Visiting Fez

"Faux Guides"

Unofficial guides sometimes offer to "show you the way" and lead visitors to shops paying them commission. Politely decline and keep walking, or book an official guide through your riad in advance.

You Will Get Lost, and That's Normal

GPS struggles in the covered alleys of the medina. Screenshot your riad's exact location and the nearest landmark gate before you set out each day.

Visiting the Tanneries Respectfully

Viewing terraces are run by the leather shops below; there is no fixed entry fee, but a small tip is expected, and you are not obliged to buy anything.

Arriving With Luggage

Cars cannot enter Fes el Bali. Most riads arrange a porter with a handcart to meet you at the nearest gate - confirm this when booking.

Beyond the Medina

Best Day Trips from Fez

Volubilis & Moulay Idriss

Morocco's best-preserved Roman ruins, with mosaics still in place, paired with the hillside holy town of Moulay Idriss nearby (about 1h15 drive).

Ifrane & the Cedar Forest

A Swiss-style mountain town, followed by a stop in the cedar forests near Azrou where Barbary macaques gather roadside (about 1h drive).

Sefrou

A smaller, quieter walled town with its own mellah and waterfalls, often called a miniature preview of Fez itself (about 30 min drive).

Good to Know

Fez Travel FAQ

How many days do you need in Fez?

Two full days cover the medina's main sights - the Chouara tannery, Al Quaraouiyine, Bou Inania. Three days lets you add the Mellah, a sunset at the Merenid Tombs, and a slower pace through the souks.

Is Fez worth visiting compared to Marrakech?

They offer different experiences. Fes el Bali feels more like a living medieval city, with artisan guilds still working by hand. Marrakech leans more toward gardens, nightlife, and modern comfort. Many travelers do both on the same trip.

Do you need a guide for the Fez medina?

Not strictly, but it helps for the first morning. Fes el Bali has more than 9,000 alleys and patchy phone signal, so a half-day official guide to get your bearings, followed by solo exploring, works well for most people.

What is Fez most famous for?

The Chouara Tannery, one of the oldest leather tanneries still in operation anywhere, and Al Quaraouiyine, founded in 859 AD and recognized as the oldest existing degree-granting university in the world.
