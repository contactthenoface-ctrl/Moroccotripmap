Tangier

Where the Atlantic meets the Mediterranean — medina, Kasbah views, Café Hafa and the road to Cap Spartel.

Best Time to Visit Apr – Jun & Sep – Nov
Ideal Stay 2 – 4 Days
Top Vibe Gateway & Strait
Nearest Airport CMN (Mohammed V)

Welcome to Morocco’s Northern Gateway

Tangier is where Africa and Europe almost touch — Atlantic on one side, Mediterranean on the other, and Spain often visible across the strait. The medina still feels layered: Kasbah walls, steep lanes, and the old international-zone stories that writers never quite stopped telling. Below, the modern city stretches along the bay with cafés, beaches and a ferry terminal that keeps the place looking outward.

Climb the Kasbah for the classic view over the port and the strait. Lose an afternoon in the medina around the Petit Socco, drink mint tea at Café Hafa with the sea below, and leave time for Cap Spartel and the Caves of Hercules when you want the coast without the city noise. Two to four nights work well if Tangier is a gateway in or out of Morocco.

Planning

Suggested 3-Day Itinerary

A relaxed schedule to see the highlights without rushing.

Morning — Kasbah & medina heights

Start at the Kasbah museum and ramparts for the port and strait view. Then drop into the medina lanes toward the Petit Socco — slower than Marrakech, still full of detail.

Afternoon — Grand Socco & American Legation

Cross the Grand Socco (Place du 9 Avril) and, if open, visit the American Legation museum — a quiet room of history in the medina edge.

Evening — Café Hafa or bay sunset

Café Hafa for mint tea on the cliff terraces, or a walk along the corniche as the lights come on over the bay.

Morning — Cap Spartel

Head west to Cap Spartel, where the Atlantic and Mediterranean meet. The lighthouse area and coastal path are the point — go early for clearer air.

Afternoon — Caves of Hercules

A short hop from Cap Spartel: the sea cave with the famous Africa-shaped opening. Combine both in one half-day rather than two separate trips.

Evening — Medina dinner

Back in town for fish or a simple Moroccan kitchen near the soccos. Tangier’s food scene is less hyped than Marrakech — good plates still exist if you ask locally.

Morning — Beach or Cap Malabata

Municipal beach for a easy sea day, or Cap Malabata for another viewpoint over the bay and the Spanish coast on a clear morning.

Afternoon — Free medina or ferry timing

Last shopping around the Grand Socco, or buffer time if you have a ferry or train to catch toward the south or toward Spain.

Evening — Departure or last Hafa tea

One more terrace if you stay the night, or an evening departure toward Chefchaouen, Fes or the airport.

Practical Tips for Visiting Tangier

Getting Around

Petit taxis cover the city; agree fares for Cap Spartel and the Caves, or book a driver for the half-day coast loop.

Dress & Etiquette

Tangier is more relaxed than inland cities, but modest clothing is still appreciated, especially when visiting the mosque. Bring a light jacket — the Atlantic breeze can be cool even in summer.

Safety & Atmosphere

Tangier is a border and port city: use normal awareness in the medina at night and around busy taxi ranks.

Day Trips

Best Day Trips from Tangier

Chefchaouen, Asilah, Tetouan and the Cap Spartel coast loop.

Chefchaouen

Blue town in the Rif, about 2–2.5 hours — better as an overnight, doable as a long day if you must.

Asilah

Atlantic white town south of Tangier — quieter walls, art and a relaxed half to full day by the sea.

Tetouan

Historic medina and Spanish influence inland from the coast — good if you want another northern city flavour.

Cap Spartel & Caves

Western headland and the Caves of Hercules — the classic half-day coast loop from Tangier, not a border run.

Good to Know

Tangier Travel FAQ

How many days should I spend in Tangier?

Two full days cover the main sights comfortably. Three or four days allow a relaxed pace plus a day trip to Marrakech or Chefchaouen.

Is Tangier safe for tourists?

Yes. Like any large city, use normal precautions. Tourist areas, the Beach and the centre are generally well-frequented and feel safe.

What currency is used and can I pay by card?

Morocco uses the dirham (MAD). Cards are widely accepted in hotels and restaurants; keep some cash for taxis, markets and smaller shops.

How do I get from Mohammed V Airport to the city centre?

The ONCF airport train is the most convenient option (about 35–45 minutes). Official taxis and private transfers are also available; agree the price or use the meter.
