Quartier Habous

A 1930s neo-Moorish neighbourhood of arcades, craft shops and quiet cafés near the Royal Palace.

Best Time to Go Morning or late afternoon
Time Needed 1 – 2 hours
Entry Free to wander
Where Habous / Nouvelle Médina

Casablanca’s Planned Medina

Built in the 1930s under the French Protectorate, the Quartier Habous (also called the Nouvelle Médina) was designed as a modern interpretation of a traditional medina. Arcaded streets, whitewashed walls and green-tiled roofs give it a calm, ordered feel that contrasts with the denser Old Medina.

It is a good place to browse crafts, leather and traditional clothing without the intensity of larger tourist markets. The Mahkama du Pacha and the nearby Royal Palace walls are part of the same ensemble.

Cafés in the main squares are ideal for mint tea. The area works well as a half-day stop between downtown and a taxi to the Corniche or the Old Medina.

Where to Eat in Quartier Habous

Habous is better known for crafts than for street food, but the main squares have cafés ideal for mint tea and a light break between shops.

  • Cafés on the main squares for mint tea and people-watching.
  • Small bakeries and pastry shops for a quick sweet stop.
  • Traditional restaurants a short walk away if you want a full meal.
  • Juice and coffee stands for a simple refreshment.
  • Hotel restaurants toward the centre if you prefer a quieter table.

This is not a food-market district like the Marché Central. Come for the architecture and crafts; use the cafés as a pause, not as the main reason for the visit.

Practical Tips for Visiting the Quartier Habous

Getting There

Taxis and most walking routes end at the square, so it's an easy landmark to give a driver. Insist on the meter (compteur) in petit taxis. Cars can't enter the medina alleys, so your riad may ask you to meet a porter at the edge.

Photos and Performers

Musicians, street offers artists and snake handlers expect a tip if you photograph them, so ask first and agree on an amount. Many travellers skip acts that use chained monkeys or snakes; you'll find plenty to watch without them.

Staying Comfortable

It gets crowded and loud, so wear your bag in front and keep your phone out of back pockets. If someone grabs your hand to start a street offers design, just pull away politely and keep walking. Unofficial guides will offer to lead you somewhere; a firm no, thank you is enough.

Good to Know

Quartier Habous FAQ

Can non-Muslims visit the Quartier Habous?

Yes. Guided tours for non-Muslims are offered several times a day (except Fridays during prayer times). Tickets are sold on site or online; arrive early in high season.

What is the best time to visit the Quartier Habous?

Morning tours are quieter and cooler. Late afternoon light is beautiful for exterior photos. Avoid Friday midday prayer when tours are limited.

Is there a dress code?

Yes. Shoulders and knees should be covered. Women may be asked to cover their hair; scarves are sometimes provided. Shoes are removed before entering the prayer hall.

How long does the visit take?

The guided tour lasts about 45–60 minutes. Allow extra time for exterior photos and the esplanade.

How much does the ticket cost?

Adult tickets are typically around 130–140 MAD. Prices can change — check the official site or the ticket office on the day.
