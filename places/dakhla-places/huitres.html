Oyster Farms

Lagoon oyster farms with tastings — a direct way to taste Dakhla’s bay.

Best Time to Go Late morning or afternoon
Time Needed 1 – 2 hours
Entry Tasting / visit fee
Where Lagoon farms

Seafood from the lagoon

Oyster farming has become part of Dakhla’s lagoon story. Several farms welcome visitors for short tours and tastings straight from the water.

It is a low-key, local experience that fits between kite sessions or boat trips. Book through your camp or a local agency when possible.

Bring sun protection; shade can be limited on the water’s edge.

What to Do at Oyster Farms, Hour by Hour

The square has three very different personalities. Ideally you'll see at least two.

Morning

Quiet coffee and orientation

Fresh orange juice or coffee at a pavement café, then a first look at the lanes into the souks before the day heats up. The best time for photos without the crowd.

Before 11 am • Easy pace
Late Afternoon

Local life and golden light

Tables fill, conversations rise, and the square becomes the natural meeting point between the two markets. Order mint tea and watch the light turn gold on the ochre walls.

4 – 7 pm • Best views
After Dark

Terrace time after the markets

A last mint tea or a simple dinner facing the square before walking back toward the lagoon shore or your riad. The atmosphere stays local and unhurried.

7 pm – Midnight • Busiest

What to Eat at the Food Stalls

Several cafés and simple restaurants face the square. For a fuller meal, step into the nearby Arab or Berber souk, or book a table in a town riad. Prices are usually lower and less pressured than in larger tourist cities.

  • Mint tea and fresh orange juice on a café terrace overlooking the square.
  • Simple Moroccan staples — tagine, couscous or grilled meats — in square-side restaurants.
  • Argan oil, amlou and local honey from stalls in the nearby souks.
  • Snail soup (babbouche) if you're feeling brave, and sheep's head if you're feeling very brave.
  • Fresh orange juice from the carts and a bag of dates or dried figs for the walk home.

Most stalls post a price board. Check it before you order, and confirm what is included with your plate so there are no surprises when the bill arrives. If it's your first evening in Dakhla and you'd rather have someone else do the choosing, a guided food tour is an easy way in.

Practical Tips for Visiting Oyster Farms

Getting There

Most taxis and walking routes meet at or near the square. It is an easy landmark to name for a driver. Petit taxis should use the meter; grands taxis and calèches need a fixed price agreed in advance.

Photos and Performers

People-watching is part of the experience. If you photograph shopkeepers, ask first. Unofficial guides may offer to lead you into the souks; a firm “no thank you” is enough.

Staying Comfortable

Keep bags in front of you in denser moments and avoid leaving phones on café tables unattended. The square itself is open and straightforward to leave in any direction.

Good to Know

Oyster Farms FAQ

Is Oyster Farms free to visit?

Yes. There is no ticket and no gate, the square is open around the clock. Where money does change hands is at the orange juice carts, the cafés and shore, the rooftop cafés and when you tip a performer.

What is the best time to go to Oyster Farms?

Late afternoon into the evening. Morning for a quiet coffee and orientation, late afternoon into the evening for the busiest local atmosphere. The square stays active after dark with café terraces.

Is Oyster Farms safe at night?

Most visitors feel comfortable. The square is well lit and busy with locals. Keep valuables secure as you would in any public space.

How long should I spend at Oyster Farms?

Plan on 30 minutes to 1.5 hours for a drink and people-watching. Use it as a base between the Arab and Berber souks and you may return several times in a day.

Can I eat at Oyster Farms?

Yes. Several cafés and simple restaurants face the square. For a fuller meal, step into the nearby souks or book a table in a town riad.
