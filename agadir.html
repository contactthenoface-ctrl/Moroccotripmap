Agadir

Wide Atlantic bay, modern corniche, hilltop Kasbah views and a gateway to Taghazout surf and Paradise Valley — Agadir is Morocco’s easy beach capital.

Best Time to Visit Apr – Jun & Sep – Nov
Ideal Stay 3 – 5 Days
Top Vibe Sunny & Beach
Nearest Airport CMN (Mohammed V)

Welcome to Morocco’s Atlantic Beach Capital

Agadir is Morocco’s big Atlantic beach city — rebuilt after the 1960 earthquake into a wide, modern resort rather than a medina maze. What you get instead is a long sandy bay, a palm-lined corniche, and a hilltop Kasbah that still watches over everything. The pace is slower than Marrakech, sunnier than the north, and built for days that end with salt on your skin and grilled fish on the plate.

Climb the Kasbah at golden hour for the classic curve of the bay. Lose a morning in Souk El Had, one of the largest markets in the country. Use the city as a base for Taghazout’s surf breaks, Paradise Valley’s palm pools, and day trips into the Anti-Atlas. Three to five nights work well if you want beach time without giving up day-trip options.

Planning

Suggested 3-Day Itinerary

A relaxed schedule to see the highlights without rushing.

Morning — Kasbah viewpoint

Drive or taxi up to the Kasbah ruins for the full sweep of the bay. The walls are modest; the panorama is the point. Go early for softer light and fewer coaches.

Afternoon — Souk El Had

One of Morocco’s largest covered markets: spices, olives, textiles, everyday life. Agree prices calmly, taste where invited, and keep valuables close in the denser aisles.

Evening — Corniche & beach sunset

Walk the seafront as the sun drops behind the bay. Cafés and hotels line the corniche; for serious fish, the port grills still win on freshness and price.

Morning — Beach or marina

Long sandy beach for a swim or a long walk; or the marina for coffee, boats and a quieter waterfront corner of the resort.

Afternoon — Taghazout or Crocoparc

North to Taghazout for surf village atmosphere (about 20–30 min), or inland to Crocoparc if travelling with family. Both need a half-day commitment with traffic.

Evening — Seafood dinner

Port-side grills or a corniche restaurant. Agadir’s strength is simple fish done well — skip the tourist menus that overpromise.

Morning — Paradise Valley or free beach

Paradise Valley’s palm gorge and natural pools reward an early start (rough road, better with a driver who knows it). Or a free morning on the sand.

Afternoon — Valley of the Birds or shops

A small city park if you want shade and a short stop, or last shopping near the corniche and souk edges.

Evening — Last bay view or travel

One more Kasbah or corniche sunset if you stay, or an evening transfer toward Marrakech or Essaouira depending on your route.

Practical Tips for Visiting Agadir

Getting Around

The beach and corniche are walkable in sections; for the Kasbah, Souk El Had or Taghazout, use taxis and agree the fare before you start.

Dress & Etiquette

Agadir is more relaxed than inland cities, but modest clothing is still appreciated, especially when visiting the mosque. Bring a light jacket — the Atlantic breeze can be cool even in summer.

Safety & Atmosphere

Agadir is a resort city: use normal awareness at night on quieter stretches of the corniche and in the denser souk aisles.

Day Trips

Best Day Trips from Agadir

Easy train or car excursions that fit comfortably into a single day.

Taghazout & surf coast

Surf village north of Agadir — reef breaks, cafés and a calmer village pace about 20–30 minutes away.

Paradise Valley

Palm-lined gorge and natural pools in the hills; best with an early start and a driver used to the track.

Crocoparc

Crocodile park and gardens south of town — an easy family half-day if you need a break from the beach.

Essaouira (longer day)

UNESCO wind city about 3–3.5 hours north; doable as a long day but better as an overnight if you can.

Good to Know

Agadir Travel FAQ

How many days should I spend in Agadir?

Two full days cover the main sights comfortably. Three or four days allow a relaxed pace plus a day trip to Marrakech or Taghazout.

Is Agadir safe for tourists?

Yes. Like any large city, use normal precautions. Tourist areas, the Beach and the centre are generally well-frequented and feel safe.

What currency is used and can I pay by card?

Morocco uses the dirham (MAD). Cards are widely accepted in hotels and restaurants; keep some cash for taxis, markets and smaller shops.

How do I get from Mohammed V Airport to the city centre?

The ONCF airport train is the most convenient option (about 35–45 minutes). Official taxis and private transfers are also available; agree the price or use the meter.
