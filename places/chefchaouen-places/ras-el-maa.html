Ras El Maa Spring

Where mountain water pours into town and everyday life gathers around it.

Best Time to GoMorning or Late Afternoon
Time Needed30 – 60 Minutes
EntryFree
WhereEast Edge of the Medina

The Spring That Made the Town Possible

Ras El Maa, literally 'head of the water', is a natural spring in the hills just outside the east gate of the medina. Cold mountain water rushes down a small stream through the old quarter, and for generations locals have used it for washing clothes and rugs.

It feels more like everyday life than a monument. Watch the stream, sit at a café by the water, or take the path that climbs from here towards the Spanish Mosque. Photograph respectfully: people are going about their day, so ask before you point a camera at someone.

Practical Tips for Visiting Ras El Maa Spring

Getting There

It is a short walk from Place Outa el Hammam through the eastern alleys, downhill to the river. Follow the sound of water.

Timing

Mornings are quiet, late afternoons pleasant. After heavy rain the stream runs faster and louder.

Good to Know

Please ask before photographing people, especially women washing clothes. Keep paths clear and mind wet stones.

Good to Know

Ras El Maa Spring FAQ

What does Ras El Maa mean?

Literally 'head of the water', named for the spring where the stream begins.

How long do you need at Ras El Maa?

30 to 60 minutes is enough for a walk, a look at the stream and a tea.

Can you drink the spring water?

Locals use it for washing. If you are unsure, stick to bottled water.

Is Ras El Maa free to visit?

Yes. It is an open, free area. You pay only for tea or food at the cafés.
