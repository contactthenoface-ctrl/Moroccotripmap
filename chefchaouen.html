Chefchaouen

The Iconic Blue Pearl of Morocco Nested in the Scenic Rif Mountains.

Best Time to Visit Mar – May & Sep – Nov
Ideal Stay 2 – 3 Days
Vibe Relaxed & Photogenic
Nearest Airport TNG (Tangier) or FES (Fez)

Welcome to the Blue Pearl

Chefchaouen is world-famous for its striking palette of blue-washed buildings tucked into the dramatic peaks of the Rif Mountains. Founded in 1471 as a fortress, this tranquil mountain town offers a peaceful retreat from Morocco's bustling imperial cities.

Wander through winding cobblestone alleys, relax in vibrant squares with mint tea, watch the sunset over the valley from the Spanish Mosque, or trek through the lush natural trails of Talassemtane National Park.

Planning

Suggested 2-Day Itinerary

A relaxed schedule to capture the best photography spots and mountain landscapes.

Morning — Place Outa el Hammam & the Kasbah

Start in the main square, visit the Kasbah and its gardens, then wander into the blue alleys while the light is soft and the streets are quiet.

Afternoon — Lost in the Blue Alleys

Take your time in the medina’s lanes, stopping at small shops and cafés. Enjoy mint tea on the square and keep the camera ready for every corner.

Evening — Sunset at the Spanish Mosque

Hike up to the Spanish Mosque for panoramic views over the blue rooftops at golden hour, then walk back down for dinner in the medina.

Morning — Akchour Waterfalls

Take an early grand taxi to Akchour for a scenic river walk through Talassemtane National Park, with the Little Waterfall or the God’s Bridge trail if you have the energy.

Afternoon — Return & Local Cheese

Head back to town in the afternoon and taste the region’s famous goat cheese, along with fresh bread and olives from a local vendor.

Evening — Rooftop Dinner

Finish the day with a traditional dinner on a rooftop terrace overlooking the medina lights.

Morning — Ras El Maa Spring

Walk to the Ras El Maa spring at the edge of the medina, where locals gather, wash and relax by the water.

Afternoon — Weavers & Handmade Crafts

Visit local weavers and craft shops to see woven blankets, wool goods and handmade products being made, and buy directly from the artisans.

Evening — Relaxed Departure

Enjoy a last mint tea in the square before heading on to your next destination.

Practical Tips for Visiting Chefchaouen

Steep Alleyways & Shoes

Wear comfortable walking shoes with good grip — the cobblestone steps and blue streets can be steep and slippery.

Photography Etiquette

Always ask permission before taking close-up photos of locals, especially elders and children in the medina.

Cash & ATMs

Most small shops, cafés, and local Dars only accept cash (Moroccan Dirham MAD). ATMs are available near Place Outa el Hammam.

Good to Know

Chefchaouen Travel FAQ

How many days should I spend in Chefchaouen?

2 to 3 days is ideal to wander the medina, catch the sunset at the Spanish Mosque, and take a day trip to the Akchour waterfalls.

Is Chefchaouen easy to reach by train or bus?

No, Chefchaouen has no train station. The easiest options are CTM buses, grand taxis, or private transfers from Tangier, Tetouan, or Fez.

Why is Chefchaouen painted blue?

Several theories exist: introduced by Jewish refugees in the 1930s symbolizing heaven, kept as a natural mosquito repellent, or maintained to keep homes cool and attract visitors.
