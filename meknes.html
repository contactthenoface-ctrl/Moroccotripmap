Bab Mansour, Place el-Hedim, Heri es-Souani and the gateway to Roman Volubilis — the Ismaili imperial city.

Meknes: Imperial Gates, Granaries and the Road to Volubilis

Meknes is the majestic imperial city of Sultan Moulay Ismail: monumental gates, immense granaries, and a medina that offers a relaxed atmosphere compared to bustling Fez. Place el-Hedim and the monumental Bab Mansour set the stage, while Heri es-Souani highlights the sheer scale of ancient royal logistics.

Two full days allow you to explore the imperial monuments and the old medina comfortably, while a third day is perfect for visiting the nearby Roman ruins of Volubilis and the holy mountain town of Moulay Idriss Zerhoun.

Planning

Suggested 3-Day Itinerary

A balanced schedule to see the imperial highlights and Roman ruins.

Morning — Bab Mansour & Place el-Hedim

Start at Place el-Hedim and admire the majestic architecture of Bab Mansour in the morning light.

Afternoon — Medina Souks

Wander through the peaceful artisan souks and discover local crafts at your own pace.

Evening — Terrace mint tea

Relax on a rooftop café overlooking the bustling square as evening falls.

Morning — Heri es-Souani & Mausoleum

Explore the massive royal granaries and stables, followed by a visit to the Mausoleum of Moulay Ismail.

Afternoon — Bou Inania & Dar Jamai

Visit the intricate Bou Inania Madrasa and explore Moroccan artifacts at the Dar Jamai Museum.

Evening — Traditional dinner

Enjoy a slow-cooked tagine in a traditional riad courtyard.

Morning — Volubilis Roman Ruins

Take a short trip out to Volubilis to walk among the ancient Roman colonnades and mosaics.

Afternoon — Moulay Idriss Zerhoun

Stop by the sacred hillside town of Moulay Idriss for panoramic views and local pastries.

Evening — Return to Meknes

A final dinner in town before your onward journey.

Practical Tips for Visiting Meknes

Getting Around

The medina and Place el-Hedim are easily explored on foot. Use petit taxis for quick trips to Heri es-Souani, and grand taxis or arranged transport for Volubilis.

Souk Bargaining

Shopping in Meknes is much calmer than in Marrakech or Fez. Friendly bargaining is welcome in the souks for local handicrafts and carpets.

Stay Hydrated

Spring and autumn offer ideal weather for walking the ruins of Volubilis and the historic ramparts. Always carry bottled water during warmer months.

Good to Know

Meknes Travel FAQ

How many days should I spend in Meknes?

2 to 3 days is ideal to explore the imperial monuments, medina, and make a half-day trip to Volubilis.

Is Meknes safe for tourists?

Yes, Meknes is very safe and much calmer than Morocco's larger tourist hubs, allowing for a relaxed travel experience.

What currency is used and can I pay by card?

Morocco uses the dirham (MAD). Cards are accepted in major hotels and restaurants, but cash is needed for small shops and taxis.

How do I get to Meknes?

Meknes is extremely well connected by the ONCF train network from Fez, Rabat, Casablanca, and Tangier.
