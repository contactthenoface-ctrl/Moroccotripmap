Les Remparts de Taroudant

Nearly 7.5 kilometers of intact 16th-century Saadian red-clay walls framing the ancient medina.

Dynasty / Era 16th Century (Saadian)
Best Tour Mode Calèche Carriage

Historical Monument

The Grand Red Walls of Souss

The majestic red walls of Taroudant are among the best-preserved medieval fortifications in North Africa. Built during the 16th century under the Saadian Dynasty, when Sultan Mohammed ash-Sheikh made Taroudant his royal capital before the conquest of Marrakech, these 7.5-kilometer earth walls enclose the entire historical medina.

Reinforced with 130 square towers and 19 monumental bastions, the ramparts are pierced by five ancient gates (Babs). Built using traditional rammed earth (pisé) mixed with local reddish lime, the walls shift color throughout the day, glowing fiery ochre during sunset against the snow-capped High Atlas peaks.

Architectural Features

  • •
    130 Square Defense Towers: Strategic watchtowers erected every 50 meters to guard against mountain tribes and foreign armies.
  • •
    5 Monumental Gates (Babs): Including Bab El-Kasbah, Bab Zorgan, Bab Targhunt, Bab Sedra, and Bab Oulad Bounouna.
  • •
    Pisé Earth Architecture: Crafted from natural raw clay, gravel, and straw, providing thermal insulation and iconic reddish color.

Exploration

The 5 Historic Gates of Taroudant

Each monumental gate served as a military fortress and commercial checkpoint for caravans arriving from the Sahara and High Atlas.

Most Famous

Bab El-Kasbah

The most ornate and royal gate, framed by orange gardens. Gives entry to the Kasbah citadel and Palace Salam.

South Gate

Bab Zorgan

Historic southern entrance leading directly to the vibrant craft markets and leather tanning workshops.

West Gate

Bab Sedra

Flanked by massive earthen bastions and gardens, offering scenic views for sunset photography tours.

East Gate

Bab Targhunt

Located near the traditional tanneries, historically used by trade caravans coming from Agadir and the coast.

North Gate

Bab Oulad Bounouna

Facing the High Atlas mountains, guarding the northern roads toward Tizi n'Test pass.

Must-Do Experience

Take a Horse-Drawn Carriage Tour

The 7.5 km wall perimeter is too vast to walk entirely on foot under the Souss sun. Hiring a calèche at Place Assarag allows you to enjoy a leisurely 1-hour ride past all 5 gates, olive groves, and bastion towers with local commentary from your driver.

Helpful Information

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you walk on top of the ramparts?

Unlike Essaouira, walking along the upper walkway of Taroudant's ramparts is generally restricted for conservation reasons. However, you can walk around the entire outer garden path or view them from rooftop terraces.

Where do I find a calèche carriage?

Carriages line up at Place Assarag and near Bab El-Kasbah. Agree on the price (around 120–150 MAD for a 1-hour full loop) before boarding.
