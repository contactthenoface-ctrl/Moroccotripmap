Church of the Assumption

Historic 16th-century Portuguese church in the heart of Mazagan — Manueline architecture and old city heritage.

Best Time to Go Morning
Time Needed 15 – 30 minutes
Entry Exterior view / Varies
Where Portuguese Medina, El Jadida

Manueline architecture in the old city

The Church of the Assumption stands as a prominent reminder of the Portuguese colonial era in Mazagan. Built in the 16th century, its distinct bell tower and architectural style reflect European religious design adapted to the North African coast.

Stroll through the compact medina lanes to admire its historic exterior structure and surrounding atmospheric stone facades.

Combine this stop with a visit to the nearby Portuguese Cistern and the old ramparts for a complete historical walking route.

Practical Tips for Visiting the Church of the Assumption

Exterior viewing

Interior access may vary based on restoration works, but the exterior and bell tower offer great architectural appreciation.

Comfortable walking shoes

The old medina streets feature stone paving, so solid footwear makes exploring much more comfortable.

Good to Know

Church of the Assumption FAQ

Can visitors enter the church?

Exterior views and the surrounding old city square are freely accessible. Interior access depends on restoration work.

What architectural style is it?

It features classic 16th-century Portuguese Manueline architectural elements built during the colonization of Mazagan.

How long does the visit take?

About 15 to 30 minutes as part of a walking tour through the Portuguese fortified city.

Is photography allowed?

Yes, exterior photography of the bell tower and surrounding medina square is fully permitted.

What else is nearby?

The Portuguese Cistern, the ramparts, and Bastion de l'Ange are just a short walk away.
