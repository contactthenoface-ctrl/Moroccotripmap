Tiznit

Morocco’s silver capital — ochre ramparts, Amazigh jewellers and a calm medina in the Souss.

Best time October – April · Thursday market
Ideal stay 1 – 2 nights
Vibe Silver craft · walls · quiet medina
Access Agadir (AGA) ≈ 1h15 · buses & grands taxis

Welcome to Tiznit — walls, silver and Souss life

Tiznit sits about 90 km south of Agadir in the Souss-Massa region. Founded as a fortified market town in the late 19th century under Sultan Hassan I, it is ringed by roughly 7 km of ochre ramparts and remains one of Morocco’s best-known centres for Amazigh silver jewellery.

The medina is compact and walkable: jewellery workshops, Place El Mechouar, historic gates and the spring known as Source Bleue (Aïn Aqdim). It is less tourist-heavy than the coast, ideal for a half-day or an overnight stop on the road toward Sidi Ifni, Guelmim or the Anti-Atlas.

Heritage

Ramparts & historic gates

Tiznit’s walls stretch about 7 km and are punctuated by round adobe towers and five principal gates, including Bab el Khemis, Bab Targa, Bab Aglou, Bab el Maader and Bab Oulad Jerrar. Late afternoon light turns the ochre clay a warm copper colour.

You can walk sections of the perimeter or simply approach the most photogenic gates. Bab Targa on the west side opens toward palm edges; Bab el Khemis is among the liveliest approaches into the medina.

Best light: late afternoon. Wear comfortable shoes — surfaces can be uneven.

Craft

Silver jewellery souk

Tiznit’s reputation rests on Amazigh silversmithing. Inside the medina, streets of workshops and shops display fibulae (tizerzai), bracelets, pendants, rings and ceremonial pieces. Some artisans still work silver in view of visitors.

Prices are often more approachable than in Marrakech or Essaouira, but quality varies — solid silver, plated pieces and industrial stock all exist. Ask about weight and purity, and take time before buying.

Thursday’s weekly market adds energy to the whole town if your dates match.

Town

Place El Mechouar

Place El Mechouar is the old central square of Tiznit — a natural meeting point between medina lanes, cafés and the flow toward the jewellery streets.

Start here to orient yourself, then branch toward the souks or a gate. It is less a monumental plaza than a lived-in town heart.

Morning for local rhythm; late day for softer photos.

Heritage

Source Bleue (Aïn Aqdim)

Aïn Aqdim — marketed as Source Bleue — is a spring tied to the stories of Tiznit’s settlement. Do not expect a dramatic turquoise pool; treat it as a heritage landmark and a quiet pause in the urban fabric.

It sits within the historic core and pairs easily with a medina walk and a stop at the silver streets.

Combine with Place El Mechouar and the jewellery souk the same morning.

Heritage

Kasbat Aghanaj

Kasbat Aghanaj is an earthen fortress within the city walls. Courtyards and lanes feel more intimate than the main commercial streets and catch beautiful light near sunset.

Access and restoration status can vary — a short exterior walk is worthwhile even if interiors are limited.

Pair with a rampart viewpoint for golden-hour photos.

Coast

Aglou beach & village

Aglou sits on the Atlantic west of Tiznit. It is a low-key coastal village and beach stop rather than a resort strip — useful for a half-day of sea air after the medina and silver streets.

Reach it by grand taxi or private car in roughly 20–30 minutes. Facilities are simple; bring what you need for a short beach walk.

Combine morning in Tiznit medina with an afternoon at Aglou if you want both craft and coast.

Practical tips for Tiznit

Getting around

The medina is walkable. Grands taxis and buses link to Agadir; taxis help for Aglou beach.

Buying silver

Compare several shops. Ask about silver purity and whether pieces are solid or plated.

When to come

October–April is milder. Thursday is the big market day.

Good to know

Tiznit FAQ

How long do I need in Tiznit?

A focused half-day covers walls, Mechouar and the silver souk. One night is ideal if you want a slower pace or a coastal add-on.

Is Tiznit only about silver?

Silver is the signature, but the ramparts, medina life and Source Bleue make a complete short visit.

Is it a good day trip from Agadir?

Yes — about 1h15 each way. Start early to have calm time in the medina.

Best day for the market?

Thursday is the traditional weekly market day and the liveliest.
