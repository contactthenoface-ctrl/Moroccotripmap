El Marsa Port

The economic heart of Laâyoune’s coast: a busy fishing harbour where daily Atlantic catches become the freshest grilled seafood in the Sahara.

Where the Ocean Feeds the Sahara

El Marsa Port is one of the most important fishing harbours in Morocco and a major economic engine for the southern provinces. Every day, coastal and artisanal boats land hundreds of tonnes of fish – sardines, octopus, cuttlefish, sole and many other Atlantic species.

The port area is lively, practical and authentic. You will see the wholesale fish market, boats unloading, and simple grills where the day’s catch is cooked on the spot. It is the best place near Laâyoune to taste truly fresh seafood.

A visit combines easily with Foum El Oued Beach (very close) and makes a perfect half-day coastal excursion from the city.

Best Moments at El Marsa Port

Early Morning

Landings & Market Energy

Arrive early to see boats returning and the wholesale market in full activity. This is the most authentic and photogenic time.

7 – 10 am • Most lively

Lunchtime

Fresh Grilled Seafood

The simple restaurants and grills around the port serve the catch of the day. Perfect for a relaxed seafood lunch with harbour views.

12 – 3 pm • Best for eating

Late Afternoon

Quieter Harbour Walk

Activity slows down. A good time for a calm stroll along the waterfront before combining with a beach visit.

4 – 6 pm • Peaceful

Practical Tips for Visiting El Marsa Port

Getting There

Grand taxis from Laâyoune city centre take about 25–30 minutes. Many visitors combine the port with Foum El Oued Beach in the same trip.

Best Time for Photos

Morning landings offer the most activity and authentic scenes. Always ask before photographing people closely.

What to Expect

This is a working commercial port, not a polished tourist marina. Dress modestly and wear comfortable shoes.

Good to Know

El Marsa Port FAQ

Is El Marsa Port open to visitors?

Yes, the surrounding area and many of the simple restaurants are accessible. Observe the wholesale market respectfully.

How far is the port from Laâyoune?

Approximately 25–30 kilometres west of the city centre (about 25–30 minutes by taxi).

What is the best time to eat at the port?

Lunchtime (12–3 pm) is ideal for the freshest fish. Morning is best to see the boats and market activity.

Is the seafood expensive?

No. Portside grills and simple restaurants offer very good value for extremely fresh fish and seafood.

Can I combine the port with Foum El Oued Beach?

Yes – they are very close to each other and make an excellent half-day or full-day coastal outing from Laâyoune.
