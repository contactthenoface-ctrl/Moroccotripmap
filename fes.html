Welcome to Fes, Morocco's Cultural Capital

Fes feels different from the moment you enter its old city. Inside Fes el-Bali, one of the world's great medieval urban areas, narrow lanes open onto quiet courtyards, neighbourhood mosques, artisan workshops and centuries-old buildings. It is a city to explore slowly, with no need to tick every sight off a list.

A trip to Fes is as much about atmosphere as monuments. You can spend the morning discovering historic madrasas, stop for a plate of fragrant tagine, watch craftsmen at work, then return to a traditional riad before heading out again in the evening. If you want to understand Morocco's history, architecture, crafts and food in one place, Fes is a natural stop on your itinerary.

Plan Your Stay

A Relaxed 3-Day Fes Itinerary

A simple first-timer route that leaves room for getting lost in the medina — because in Fes, that is often part of the experience.

Morning — Start at Bab Bou Jeloud

Enter the medina early while the lanes are calmer. Follow the main streets towards the historic heart, stopping at small bakeries, spice shops and artisan stalls along the way.

Afternoon — Madrasa & Souks

Visit Bou Inania Madrasa and continue through the souks toward the Nejjarine area. Take your time rather than trying to cover every lane.

Evening — Sunset over the Medina

Head to a rooftop for tea and a view over the rooftops, then enjoy dinner in or around the medina.

Morning — Fes's Historic Institutions

Explore the city's historic learning and religious architecture, including the area around Al Quaraouiyine and the old neighbourhoods surrounding it.

Afternoon — Leather & Woodwork

See the traditional tanneries, browse leather workshops and visit the Nejjarine Museum. Fes is one of the best places in Morocco to see crafts still made by hand.

Evening — Slow Dinner

Try a traditional Moroccan dinner and leave some time for a quiet walk around the illuminated medina streets.

Morning — Choose Your Day Trip

Pick a trip based on your interests: Volubilis and Moulay Idriss for history, Meknes for imperial architecture, or Ifrane and the Middle Atlas for a change of scenery.

Afternoon — Return to Fes

Allow enough time to return without rushing. If you stay in the city, use the afternoon for shopping or a final visit to a favourite café.

Evening — One Last Rooftop

Finish with mint tea and a final view of Fes as the lights come on across the medina.

Eat & Drink

What to Eat in Fes

Fes is a rewarding city for food lovers, from simple medina kitchens to polished Moroccan dining rooms.

Fassi Cuisine

Look for dishes such as pastilla, slow-cooked tagines, harira and seasonal salads. Fassi cooking is often known for its balance of sweet, savoury and fragrant spices.

Medina Food Stops

Follow your appetite through the medina: bakeries, soup counters, grilled meats, olives, dates and fresh juices can make an excellent informal lunch.

Rooftop Tea

For a slower evening, choose a rooftop terrace overlooking the old city and order mint tea alongside a traditional dinner.

Cooking Experiences

A local cooking class or market visit can be a fun way to understand the ingredients and techniques behind Moroccan home cooking.

Sweet Treats

Try Moroccan pastries, chebakia and almond-based sweets with coffee or tea when you need a break from sightseeing.

What to Order

If it is your first visit, ask your host or server what is freshly prepared that day. Fes rewards curiosity more than a fixed checklist.

Practical Tips for Visiting Fes

Wear Comfortable Shoes

The old city is best explored on foot. Expect uneven paving, steps and plenty of walking.

Stay Inside or Near the Medina

A riad gives you a more immersive experience, while staying just outside the medina can be easier if you prefer taxis and wider roads.

Use a Local Guide if You Want Context

Fes has many layers of history and a guide can add context, especially on a first visit. If you go independently, save your accommodation location offline.

Carry Some Cash

Keep Moroccan dirhams for small purchases, cafés, taxis and market stalls even if your hotel or larger restaurant accepts cards.

Beyond the City

Best Day Trips from Fes

Fes is also a useful base for exploring the history and landscapes of northern and central Morocco.

Volubilis

Explore the Roman ruins, mosaics and open landscapes of one of Morocco's best-known archaeological sites.

Moulay Idriss

A hillside pilgrimage town with whitewashed streets and views across the surrounding countryside.

Meknes

Combine your Fes stay with a visit to another imperial city, known for monumental gates and historic architecture.

Ifrane & Middle Atlas

Head into the Middle Atlas for cedar forests, cooler air and a completely different landscape from the medina.

Good to Know

Fes Travel FAQ

How many days should I spend in Fes?

Two full days can cover the main sights, but three days gives you time to explore the medina without rushing and add a nearby day trip.

Is Fes a good destination for a first trip to Morocco?

Yes, especially if you are interested in history, architecture, crafts and Moroccan food. The medina is intense and busy, so a slower pace makes the experience easier.

What is the best area to stay in Fes?

Stay in or near Fes el-Bali if you want atmosphere and easy access to the historic sights. Ville Nouvelle is more convenient if you prefer modern hotels, wider roads and easier vehicle access.

Can I visit the Fes medina without a guide?

You can explore independently, although the medina is large and easy to navigate imperfectly. A guide can be useful when you want deeper historical context or help discovering less obvious places.

What should I eat in Fes?

Try Fassi specialities such as pastilla and traditional tagines, along with Moroccan salads, soups, pastries and mint tea. Ask locally what is fresh that day.

What are the best day trips from Fes?

Volubilis, Moulay Idriss, Meknes and Ifrane are among the easiest additions to a Fes itinerary, depending on whether you prefer archaeology, history or nature.
