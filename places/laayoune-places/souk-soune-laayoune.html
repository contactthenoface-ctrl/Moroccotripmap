The Everyday Heart of Sahrawi Commerce

Souk Soune is one of the most important fishing harbours in Morocco and a major economic engine for the southern provinces. Every day, coastal and artisanal boats land hundreds of tonnes of fish – sardines, octopus, cuttlefish, sole and many other Atlantic species.

You will find stalls and small shops offering Sahrawi silver jewellery with geometric designs, leather goods made from camel hide, the distinctive colourful melhfa (the traditional women’s wrap), textiles, spices, and practical household items.

It is one of the best places in the city to observe authentic daily life, practise a little bargaining, and bring home meaningful souvenirs that reflect the culture of the southern provinces.

Practical Tips for Visiting Souk Soune

Getting There

Polite bargaining is expected for crafts and non-fixed-price items. Smile, take your time, and never feel pressured. Asking permission before photographing people is always appreciated.

What to Buy

Look for genuine Sahrawi silver jewellery, camel-leather items, and the colourful melhfa. These make the most meaningful and authentic souvenirs from Laâyoune.

Getting There & Safety

The market is in central Laâyoune and easily reached on foot or by petit taxi from Place Mechouar. It is a normal local market – busy but generally safe. Keep valuables secure as in any crowded place.

Good to Know

Souk Soune FAQ

Is Souk Soune open to visitors?

Yes, it is a public traditional market open to everyone at no charge.

What are the best things to buy?

Sahrawi silver jewellery, camel-leather goods, and the colourful melhfa fabrics are the most characteristic and authentic purchases.

Do I need to bargain?

Afternoon (12–3 pm) is ideal for the freshest fish. Morning is best to see the boats and market activity.

Is the market safe for tourists?

Yes. It is a busy local market. Use normal big-city precautions (secure bags, be aware of your surroundings) and you will be fine.

How long should I spend there?

1 to 2 hours is usually enough to explore, take photos, and make a few purchases at a relaxed pace.
